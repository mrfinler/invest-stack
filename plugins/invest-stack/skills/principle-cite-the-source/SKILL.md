# Cite the Source

- **Open it.** A search snippet is not a source. Fetch the page and read the part you cite.
- **Prefer the origin.** SEC filing over a news story about it. The company's earnings release over a recap. The Fed's statement over commentary. Reuters, AP, Bloomberg, CNBC, WSJ, FT, company IR and SEC EDGAR rank above blogs and aggregators. Social posts only when the post itself is the news.
- **Date it.** Write the publish date in `sources`. A story older than the edition window is background, not news, and says so.
- **Paraphrase.** Retell in Thai in your own sentences. Quote at most one short phrase per source, under 15 words, only when the exact wording matters (a guidance number, a Fed sentence). Never reproduce paragraphs.
- **No borrowed images.** Do not copy, hotlink or embed a publisher's photo, chart or logo. Link the article. Draw your own figure from the numbers.
- **Disagreement is information.** When two good sources disagree, report both and say which you lean on and why.
